# Cold email sequence — Conversational framework

Outbound sequence for ecommerce managers, heads of marketplaces and marketplace
managers. Follows the Conversational Cold Email Kit structure (James Laurain):
a short peer opener, three genuinely useful tips with no pitch, then a
"Did that work?" breakup. The MAZ positioning (embedded partner, depth of
work, data + AI) is never pitched in the emails — it's demonstrated by the
tips, and delivered properly in the reply conversation.

**The rules the framework lives or dies by:**

- Email 1 has no signature, no company name, no pitch. Just a first name.
  It must look off-the-cuff, like a note from a peer.
- Emails 2–4 each give away one tip the reader can act on in under 15 minutes
  with tools they already have. No CTA, no "book a call".
- Never write "you might have missed my last email" or "I know you're busy".
  Approach every email as if it's the first one you've sent them.
- Full signature appears only on the breakup email.
- Keep it conversational — short words, short sentences.
- Space the tip emails roughly a week apart.

**Where the positioning lives:** when someone replies, that conversation is
where the embedded-partner story goes — named analyst in the account daily,
every change documented (what and why), working inside their data and systems,
"anything we build stays yours", data first / AI where it helps. The tips
below are deliberately drawn from that methodology, so the pitch is a natural
continuation, not a switch.

## Email 1 — the peer opener (Day 1)

This one is built per-prospect. The subject is a real, specific observation
with a trailing dash; the body is two sentences and a first name.

**Subject patterns:**

- `Just saw your Sponsored Brands ad for {{product}} -`
- `Just saw {{brand}}'s new listing on amazon.co.uk -`
- `Noticed {{product}} moved up for "{{keyword}}" -`

**Body:**

Hey {{first_name}},

I manage Amazon accounts too. How's {{product}} converting since
{{the thing you observed — the new main image / the price change / Prime Day}}?

- Calum

**Notes:** the observation must be real — an ad you actually saw, a listing
change you actually noticed. The question should be answerable in one line,
close to yes/no. Never "how's business going?". No signature block, no
company name, nothing else.

## Email 2 — tip: spend concentration (Day ~5)

**Subject:** Most of your ad spend is probably hiding in ~20 search terms

**Body:**

Hey {{first_name}} -

Have you tried pulling a 60-day search term report and sorting it by spend?
In most accounts, around 20 terms carry the bulk of the budget.

Split those into their own campaigns and watch them by hand. Let rules mind
the long tail. It's worked really well across the accounts we run.

Let me know if you want a hand pulling the report.

- Calum

---

## Email 3 — tip: the change log (Day ~12)

**Subject:** A 15-minute fix for "what did that change do?"

**Body:**

Hey {{first_name}} -

Have you tried keeping a plain change log for the account? One sheet: date,
what changed (bid, price, main image), and why.

Next time someone asks "what did that change actually do", you line the log
up against sales and search terms instead of losing a Friday afternoon to a
spreadsheet. And it compounds — every change you record makes the next answer
quicker.

Happy to send over the exact columns we use.

- Calum

---

## Email 4 — tip: measurement windows (Day ~19)

**Subject:** Why day-2 verdicts wreck Amazon tests

**Body:**

Hey {{first_name}} -

Have you tried holding off judging any bid or budget change until day 7?
Amazon's attribution lag means day-2 numbers nearly always read wrong, so
accounts end up thrashing — change, panic, revert.

We also cap big strategy shifts at one or two a fortnight, so the data stays
clean enough to actually read. It's made our calls much easier to trust.

Let me know if you want the measurement windows we use per campaign type.

- Calum

---

## Email 5 — the breakup (Day ~26)

**Subject:** Did that work?

**Body:**

Hey {{first_name}},

Hopefully you're getting cleaner reads on what each change is actually doing
from those tips (did you get a chance to try any?).

I've got a fair few more — they come straight from how we run accounts day to
day — and I'd happily talk them through. But I get it if you're not
interested.

Could you do me a favour and just let me know if you'd rather I didn't follow
up? I'd hate to be a bother.

Thanks, and good luck with Q4 prep!

Calum
{{full signature — MAZ, wearemaz.com, LinkedIn}}

## When they reply

The reply conversation (or the first call) is where the positioning from the
Super Ninja proposal comes in:

- The depth story: a named analyst in the account every business day; every
  change documented — what we changed and why. (The exact gap that makes
  people leave their agency: not enough detail, and flagged things never
  actioned.)
- The embedded model: we work inside the systems you're building — your
  database, dashboards, reporting — and help define what PPC needs from them.
  Your team ↔ our team ↔ your data and systems.
- The change-and-impact view: the change log tip, grown up — every change on
  one axis, outcomes on the other, built on the brand's own data.
- Ownership: anything we build together is built into your systems and stays
  yours.
- AI stance: AI is not the goal, better decisions are. No bidding algorithm
  nobody can interrogate.
- Proof, if asked: nine years, eight-figure spend managed; one US consumables
  brand $10k → $300k monthly ad-driven sales over two years at 22.8% ACoS;
  dashboard-spec work for another 8-figure brand.

**Targeting:** unchanged — prospects with a visible signal they're building
internally (a data hire, "dashboard" in a job post, tool sprawl) are the ones
the embedded conversation lands with.
