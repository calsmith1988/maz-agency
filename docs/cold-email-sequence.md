# Cold email sequence — Conversational framework

Outbound sequence sent via Instantly to founders, CEOs, ecommerce directors,
heads of ecommerce and marketplace directors. The sequence leads into the
**eCommerce AI Build Workshop** (3 hours, private, online, up to 6 people,
£1,250 + VAT) as the entry offer.

Follows the Conversational Cold Email Kit structure (James Laurain): a short
peer opener, three genuinely useful tips with no pitch, then a "Did that
work?" breakup. The offer is never pitched in the emails — each tip is a
15-minute miniature of the workshop (existing data + AI tools they already
have + a useful output), so the emails demonstrate the capability. The
workshop is offered in the reply conversation.

**The rules the framework lives or dies by:**

- Email 1 has no signature, no company name, no pitch. Just a first name.
  It must look off-the-cuff, like a note from a peer.
- Emails 2–4 each give away one tip the reader can act on in under 15 minutes
  with tools they already have. No CTA, no "book a call".
- Never write "you might have missed my last email" or "I know you're busy".
  Approach every email as if it's the first one you've sent them.
- Full signature appears only on the breakup email.
- Keep it conversational — short words, short sentences.
- Space the tip emails roughly a week apart.
- Plain text, no links, no images — for the framework and for deliverability.

## Email 1 — the peer opener (Day 1)

Works without enrichment, and the AI question self-selects for the workshop
buyer: everyone on this list has given (or is thinking about giving) their
team AI access, and almost nobody thinks it has stuck.

**Subject:** `quick ecom + ai question -`

**Body:**

Hey {{first_name}},

We use AI across our ecommerce work too. Has it actually stuck with your
team yet, or are they still mostly just chatting with it?

Calum

**Alternative first lines (same plain register):**

- "We've been putting AI to work in our own ecommerce ops too."
- "We've been building AI into how we run ecommerce accounts too."

**Alternative (with a `{{product}}` enrichment field, for Amazon-heavy
lists):**

**Subject:** `saw {{product}} on amazon -`

Hey {{first_name}},

I run Amazon accounts too. Is {{product}} still your main seller, or has
something overtaken it?

Calum

**Notes:** the question should be answerable in one line, close to yes/no.
Never "how's business going?". No signature block, no company name, nothing
else.

## Email 2 — tip: your exports + AI (Day ~5)

**Subject:** claude + your search term report -

**Body:**

Hey {{first_name}} -

Have you tried exporting a 60-day search term report and dropping it into
Claude? Ask it: "which terms is spend rising on while conversion falls, and
what is that costing per week?"

Five minutes, and it works with any export — business reports, returns data,
whatever you've got.

Let me know if you want the exact prompt we use.

- Calum

## Email 3 — tip: reviews into themes (Day ~12)

**Subject:** 200 reviews into 5 themes, in ten minutes

**Body:**

Hey {{first_name}} -

Have you tried pasting your last couple of hundred reviews (or return
comments) into an AI chat and asking it to cluster them into themes, with
counts and example quotes?

Most teams can feel what the top complaint is. This shows it, with numbers.
Same move works for support tickets and listing Q&A.

Happy to share the prompt if useful.

- Calum

## Email 4 — tip: prototype the manual report (Day ~19)

**Subject:** "why do we still do this manually?"

**Body:**

Hey {{first_name}} -

Pick one weekly report someone builds by hand. Write down four things: where
the data lives, the logic they apply, what the output looks like, and who
acts on it.

Then give an AI the raw exports and that logic, and ask it for the output.
Most teams realise they've just prototyped an internal tool — no developer
involved.

Let me know if you want an example to copy.

- Calum

## When they reply

The reply conversation is where the offer lives. The shape of it:

- Their situation, in their words first. The likely admission: the team has
  AI access, usage is patchy, nothing structural has changed. That is the
  offer's opening line: **an AI licence isn't an AI strategy.**
- The reframe: the gap isn't tools, it's knowing where and how to apply them.
  The people closest to the problems (marketplace, trading, inventory, CX)
  already know which reports are painful and which tools they wish existed.
- The framework the workshop teaches: **Problem → Data → Logic → Output →
  Action** — moving the team from "how can I use Claude?" to "why do we
  still do this manually?" (Email 4 is a taste of exactly this.)
- The entry offer: **eCommerce AI Build Workshop** — 3 hours, private,
  online, up to 6 people, £1,250 + VAT. A little context, a practical
  framework, then build something real on a use case the company picks.
- The outcomes: work faster, decide faster, build faster — plus one working
  build and a team that sees AI as a capability, not a chat window.
- Why us, if asked: ecommerce knowledge + AI building + systems thinking.
  Years working with ecommerce and marketplace teams (Amazon Ads, Ads API,
  SP-API, reporting, dashboards), hundreds of hours building real systems
  around ecommerce data with modern AI tools.
- The ladder beyond, only if they ask: more team sessions, AI implementation
  sprints, embedded AI support, and specialist Amazon advertising services.

## Appendix — alternative PPC tip track

For segments where the destination is the Amazon Ads / embedded partnership
offer rather than the workshop, swap Emails 2–4 for these. Same rules.

**Tip A — spend concentration:** Subject: `Most of your ad spend is probably
hiding in ~20 search terms`. Pull a 60-day search term report, sort by spend;
~20 terms usually carry the budget. Split them into their own campaigns and
watch them by hand; let rules mind the long tail. Close: "Let me know if you
want a hand pulling the report."

**Tip B — the change log:** Subject: `A 15-minute fix for "what did that
change do?"`. One sheet: date, what changed (bid, price, main image), and
why. Line it up against sales and search terms instead of losing a Friday
afternoon to a spreadsheet; it compounds. Close: "Happy to send over the
exact columns we use."

**Tip C — measurement windows:** Subject: `Why day-2 verdicts wreck Amazon
tests`. Hold verdicts until day 7 (attribution lag); cap big strategy shifts
at one or two a fortnight so the data stays readable. Close: "Let me know if
you want the measurement windows we use per campaign type."

In the reply conversation for this track, lead with the embedded-partner
positioning from the Super Ninja proposal: a named analyst in the account
every business day, every change documented (what and why), working inside
the client's data and systems, "anything we build stays yours", data first /
AI where it helps.
