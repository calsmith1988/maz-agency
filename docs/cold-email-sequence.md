# Cold email sequence — Embedded Amazon Partnership

Outbound sequence for ecommerce managers, heads of marketplaces and marketplace
managers. Built on the positioning in the Super Ninja proposal
(`src/pages/super-ninja-proposal-x7k2m9.astro`) and the site's "Why MAZ" block.

Five emails over ~18 days. One idea per email: pain → model → proof → AI →
breakup. Every claim comes from the proposal or the site.

**Targeting:** the reader owns the agency relationship and feels the depth
problem personally. Strong triggers: a data hire, a "dashboard" or internal
reporting mention on LinkedIn, visible tool sprawl.

**Personalisation:** swap the first line of Email 1 for something
account-specific where possible (category, a visible listing change, a
Sponsored Brands campaign). Emails 2–5 work as-is.

**CTA:** always the site's low-friction offer — thirty minutes, live, no deck,
no hard sell. Don't escalate to "book a demo" language.

---

## Email 1 — the depth problem (Day 1)

**Subject:** How deep does your Amazon agency actually go?
**Alt subject:** What changed, and why?

Hi {{first_name}},

Quick question about how {{brand}}'s Amazon account is being managed.

When your agency sends its update, does it tell you what they changed and
why — or does it just describe what happened?

We hear the same story from a lot of ecommerce and marketplace managers:
performance looks fine on the surface, but nobody is going deep into the
account. Search terms don't get properly worked. Things get flagged on a call,
and three calls later nothing has been done about them.

We run it differently. A named analyst in the account every business day —
bids, budgets, placements, search terms — with every change documented: what
we did, and the reason behind it.

Happy to show you what that looks like against {{brand}}'s account. Thirty
minutes, live, no deck.

Calum

## Email 2 — the embedded model (Day 4)

**Subject:** Most agencies stop at the edge of the account

Hi {{first_name}},

Most Amazon agencies work at arm's length. You get campaign management, a
report, and a monthly call. Your data, your internal tools and the people
making decisions all sit on the other side of the fence — and context dies in
the handovers.

We work as an embedded partner instead. We manage Amazon Ads day to day, and
we work inside the systems you're building — your database, your dashboards,
your reporting — helping define what PPC actually needs from them.

Instead of: brand → agency → report → brand
It becomes: your team ↔ our team ↔ your data and systems

And one principle underneath it: anything we build together is built into your
systems and stays yours — during the work and after it.

If {{brand}} is building anything internally around marketplace data, this is
probably a conversation worth having.

Calum

## Email 3 — concrete proof (Day 8)

**Subject:** "What did that change do?"

Hi {{first_name}},

A question that eats hours in most marketplace teams: we changed a bid, a
price, a main image — what did it actually do?

Usually the answer lives in a spreadsheet someone builds on a Friday
afternoon. So one of the first things we build with partners is a
change-and-impact view: every change on one axis, outcomes on the other —
search terms, rank, sales. Built on the brand's own data, and it compounds —
every change recorded makes the next answer sharper.

That sits on top of the actual work: nine years managing Amazon Ads, eight
figures of spend. One US consumables brand went from $10k to $300k in monthly
ad-driven sales over two years at a 22.8% ACoS. We've also helped another
8-figure brand spec their internal Amazon dashboard — which metrics, which API
endpoints, attribution windows.

Worth thirty minutes?

Calum

## Email 4 — the AI angle (Day 13)

**Subject:** AI won't fix a shallow agency

Hi {{first_name}},

Every agency deck now has an AI slide. Here's our plainer version.

AI is not the goal. Better decisions are. AI is genuinely useful for the grunt
work — pulling reports together, summarising, flagging unusual changes,
investigating a dip before a human even looks. We build that into how we
manage accounts, on top of your own data — not on top of someone else's
platform.

What it doesn't replace: the people who understand the products, the market
and the strategy. And what we won't do is hand your account to a bidding
algorithm nobody can interrogate.

Good data + Amazon expertise + useful AI = better decisions. That's the whole
thesis.

If every agency pitching you right now says "AI-powered", happy to show you
what it looks like when it's actually wired into the day-to-day work.

Calum

## Email 5 — breakup (Day 18)

**Subject:** Closing the loop

Hi {{first_name}},

Last one from me, and I'll keep it short.

One test, whoever manages your Amazon: open the last update they sent. Does it
say what they changed and why? And did the things flagged a month ago actually
get done?

If yes — you're in good hands, and I'd stick with them.

If not: named analyst in the account daily, embedded in your data and systems,
and everything we build stays yours. Thirty minutes and I'll show you what
we'd prioritise first on {{brand}}.

Either way, good luck with Q4 prep.

Calum
