Positions, organic and sponsored, come from the rank tracker you already run, folded into the same database. Share and click data comes from your own Search Query Performance feed, which measures what every real customer saw and did rather than one synthetic search. The join between the two is what this page is: position is the proxy, measured share is the truth, and the handover chart needs both.

How it is pieced together. Everything is reshaped onto one spine: keyword, marketplace, week. The tracker's daily positions average into a weekly rank; fast, but an estimate from one synthetic search a day. Search Query Performance arrives weekly with the query's total impressions, clicks and purchases and your share of each; slower, but measured from every real customer. The ads reports say which of those clicks were bought and at what cost. Organic clicks are the remainder, total clicks minus paid clicks: no Amazon report contains that number, it only exists once the feeds share a database. The phases (building, holding, slipping) are plain rules over those columns, not a model. Three tables, one join key, one subtraction, running inside your own system.
